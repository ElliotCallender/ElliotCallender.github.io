When I was somewhere between the age of 6 and 12 years, I remember considering the trolley problem while riding in a car. It was immediately clear to me that utilitarianism makes more sense than deontology (though I hadn't heard those words at the time) because choosing not to pull the lever would still be a choice. It would take much, much longer for me to consider actually applying this to reality.

By the time I was 16yo, I had decided I was going to homestead in Alaska. This was a desperate attempt to escape the emotional and intellectual claustrophobia, a suffocating prisoner mentality, from attending a poorly-funded school in the southern US. School was by far the most traumatic event in my life.

At this time, I was also considering how I could help the world. What's the most efficient way to improve everything? Then I watched a Rob Miles AI video and started reading LessWrong.

It was, again, quickly apparent to me that AI alignment was an extremely hard problem, but I wasn't aware of how immensely difficult. By 18yo I was considering dropping out of my math degree; just before my 20th birthday, I left my senior year of college to work on more stimulating things.

I remember reading John Wentworth's natural abstractions work and thinking "why are we still studying unbounded agents when computational boundedness is what necessitates abstraction?" After realizing that a useful notion of computational complexity for agents is extremely hard (and that others have worked on AI safety for two decades without coming close to solving it), I started thinking about human cognition enhancement. Could we add neural stem cells to increase brain mass? (not yet) What about other approaches?

After a bit of looking, it seemed to me that strong human intelligence amplification was horrendously overlooked. For example, sphingolipid precursors, BDNF modulators, elastic high-density BCIs with commensurate software, and ultrasonically-scanned AI-classified mind uploading all seem capital-impoverished, though I haven't studied enough acoustic physics to say with confidence that [crystal-or-superfluid]-infiltrated sonic brainscans could work with 20nm resolution. (Can you tell I find these topics more intellectually stimulating than AI safety technical research?)

Then I met Michael Trazzi at a Stop The AI Race march. He was planning another demonstration starting in 9 days where we'd stay in front of AI labs until they lobbbied the US government for an international development pause. A coalition of employees from multiple labs could have enormous leverage, but most employees seemed not to take AI existential risk seriously (or at least have the conviction to act on such a belief).

\So I spend most of my waking hours outside of OpenAI. The recent HuggingFace (and other) breaches make pausing more politically feasible. I hope we do pause so I can return to more fun activities.\
